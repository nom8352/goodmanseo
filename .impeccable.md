## Previous Design Context

### Users
Korean-speaking small business owners in Australia, especially Sydney and nearby regions. They are usually busy owner-operators, not very technical, and want their business to look trustworthy online without having to understand every marketing or SEO detail.

Their job to be done is practical: see how their Google Business Profile, website, Instagram, and Facebook look to customers; understand what is missing; and decide what to fix first so inquiries become easier.

### Brand Personality
Friendly, practical, trustworthy.

The tone should feel like a calm partner helping a business owner make sense of their online presence, not a pushy agency pitch. The emotional goal is confidence and relief: "now I know where to start."

### Aesthetic Direction
Clear, concise, and approachable. The current site uses a dark, glassy visual system with Pretendard for Korean UI text, Outfit for display headings, lime and light-blue accents, rounded cards, pill CTAs, and motion/3D as supporting polish.

Design should stay readable and service-focused, not decorative for its own sake. Copy should be short, direct, and easy to scan on desktop and mobile. Avoid overly promotional language, heavy jargon, and long explanations. Keep the same practical tone across the website, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, blog, and card-news assets.

Anti-references: generic website-agency landing pages, abstract "online structure" consulting language, salesy social ads, and near-duplicate card-news design options that only change surface styling.

### Design Principles
- Lead with the customer-visible outcome: what customers see online, what is missing, and what to fix first.
- Keep one idea per section or line whenever possible; make pages scannable for busy small-business owners.
- Use short, human Korean sentences; avoid jargon unless it is immediately explained.
- Keep pricing visible and easy to compare, but secondary to the problem, fit, and next action.
- Preserve the Google + website + Instagram + Facebook "seen together" story across every page and social asset.
- Make every word earn its place; remove decorative copy that does not help the owner decide.
- For visual options, vary layout and hierarchy meaningfully instead of producing superficial variants.
